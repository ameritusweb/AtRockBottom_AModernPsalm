# At Rock Bottom
**a modern psalm**

> *"He brought me up out of the pit of destruction, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, making my footsteps firm."* — Psalm 40:2  

## Overview

**At Rock Bottom** is a contemporary worship song written in a single sitting during a moment of crisis; stranded at an understaffed auto parts store with nowhere to go and no clear way forward. What emerged was not a carefully crafted composition, but an unbroken prayer: honest, direct, and repetitive, like the Psalms themselves.

The song testifies to a profound truth: God meets us at our lowest point. Not after we've climbed out, not when we've gotten ourselves together, but *at rock bottom*—right there in the pit.

## Musical Characteristics

### The Pentatonic Foundation
The melody of every verse, tag, and finale line uses **E♭ minor pentatonic** (E♭ G♭ A♭ B♭ D♭), a rare choice in contemporary worship music. This five-note scale creates:
- No harsh dissonances—every note flows naturally
- A chant-like, meditative quality
- Simplicity that serves the prayer, not the performance
- Accessibility for congregational singing

The one deliberate exception is **the chant** (mm. 93–110), which steps outside the six-flat world entirely—see below.

### The Core Motifs
The song is built from four melodic shapes:
- **Cadence**: A♭–B♭–G♭–E♭ ("at rock bottom")
- **Answer**: D♭–B♭–A♭–E♭ ("there you'll find me")
- **Rise**: G♭–A♭–B♭ (verse lines climbing)
- **Recite**: B♭–D♭ (psalm-tone recitation)

Once you know these four shapes, the note tables below become easy to read—almost every line is one of them, or two of them joined.

### Harmonic Structure
**Main progression**: one chord per bar, looping continuously:

```
| E♭m | C♭ | G♭ | D♭ |
```

The loop runs unbroken from measure 1 through measure 92, pauses for the chant, then **restarts on E♭m at measure 112** and runs to measure 147. The loop ends on D♭ (the dominant), not the tonic, creating continuous forward pull—the song wants to keep going, like a pilgrimage that doesn't stop.

**The chant harmony** (mm. 93–110) leaves E♭ minor for a parallel world of naturals. Each chant line spans five bars:

```
| E♭m | Em  Cmaj7 | Cmaj7  Am7  D | Em  Cmaj7 | Cmaj7  Am7  B7 |
```

…and each "at rock bottom" response lands back on E♭m. The half-step slip between E♭m and Em makes the chant feel like a held breath in another room before the door opens again.

**The ending** (mm. 148–151): four bars of E♭m, held. The song finally lands on the tonic—on the rock, at rock bottom, because that's where the foundation was found.

### The Momentum
Several elements create the song's distinctive processional quality:
- **Stepwise motion**: most melodic moves are 3 semitones or less
- **Unresolved phrases**: many lines hang on D♭ or B♭, pulling forward
- **Circular harmony**: the progression never fully "lands" until the end
- **Speech rhythm**: one note per syllable, flowing like spoken prayer
- **Tempo acceleration**: from 90 BPM (meditative) to 110 BPM (being lifted up)

## The Story

This song was written in one continuous sitting, with no pauses, during what felt like rock bottom; stranded and helpless at an auto parts store with no resolution in sight. The momentum in the music reflects the continuous, urgent nature of that prayer. It couldn't stop until it reached the answer: "there you'll find me."

The song is not about staying at rock bottom. It's about discovering that God is *present* there—not waiting for you to climb out first, but meeting you in the pit itself.

## Performance Notes

### For Worship Leaders
- This song works best in intimate settings—prayer rooms, small gatherings, contemplative services
- The repetition is intentional; let it build through cycles
- The narrow, mostly pentatonic melody is accessible to most singers
- Consider starting a cappella or with minimal instrumentation (pad, fingerpicked guitar)
- Don't rush the 90 BPM section—let it breathe
- The chant (mm. 93–110) can be sung by the congregation as a round of trust
- Honor the bar of silence at measure 111—it's part of the song
- The tempo change at measure 112 should feel like a surge of hope, not just faster

### Pairing with "In Christ Alone" (Brian Littrell)

- His B♭ chord (his tonic) = your V chord (dominant to E♭m)

One is the declaration; the other is where that declaration is tested and proven.

## Theological Notes

This song belongs to the tradition of the **lament psalms**:
- Honest about the darkness (Psalm 88, 130, 142)
- Direct address to God from the pit (Psalm 40, 69)
- Testimony of deliverance (Psalm 18, 34)

It was written the way those psalms were written—not as polished hymns crafted at a desk, but as real-time prayers with the urgency and momentum of desperation.

The refrain "at rock bottom / there you'll find me" is both:
1. **Testimony**: That's where God met me
2. **Invitation**: That's where God will meet you too

And like many lament psalms, the song ends with the answer arriving: the closing lines shift from the singer's voice to God's—"Find it in Me… I've brought you up."
